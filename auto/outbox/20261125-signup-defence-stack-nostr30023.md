# A layered signup defence for small SaaS teams

channel: nostr30023
kind: longform

When building a small SaaS product, one of the most critical components to consider is the signup process. This is the first point of contact between your service and potential users, and it's essential to ensure that it's secure and resistant to abuse. Abuse can come in many forms, including fake signups, API key farming, and server-side token verification bypassing. To combat these issues, it's crucial to implement a layered defence strategy that includes multiple checks and verifications.

## Layer 1: Proof of Work on the Form
The first layer of defence is to implement a proof of work check on the signup form. This check requires the user's browser to perform a small amount of computational work before submitting the form, which helps to prevent automated scripts from signing up. The proof of work check is invisible to the user and doesn't require any additional input or interaction. By adding this check, you can significantly reduce the number of fake signups and make it more difficult for attackers to abuse your service.

## Layer 2: Server-Side Verify with Strict Failure Handling
The second layer of defence is to implement a server-side verify check. This check involves verifying the user's input data, such as their email address or phone number, to ensure that it's valid and legitimate. The server-side verify check should be done using a secure protocol, such as HTTPS, and should include strict failure handling to prevent attackers from bypassing the check. This can include returning specific error codes or messages to indicate that the verification failed.

## Layer 3: Limits per Address and Account
The third layer of defence is to implement rate limits per address and account. This involves limiting the number of signups or requests that can be made from a single IP address or account within a certain time period. By implementing these limits, you can prevent attackers from making multiple signups or requests in a short amount of time, which can help to reduce abuse. It's also essential to log and monitor these limits to detect and respond to potential abuse.

## Layer 4: Confirmation and Passkeys
The fourth layer of defence is to implement confirmation and passkeys. This involves sending a confirmation email or message to the user's email address or phone number to verify their identity. Once the user confirms their identity, a passkey can be generated and sent to their device. The passkey can then be used to authenticate the user and grant access to the service. By implementing confirmation and passkeys, you can add an additional layer of security and make it more difficult for attackers to gain unauthorized access.

## Risk Scores and Adaptive Difficulty
Another essential component of a layered defence strategy is to use risk scores and adaptive difficulty. Risk scores involve assigning a score to each user based on their behavior and activity, such as the number of signups or requests made within a certain time period. Adaptive difficulty involves adjusting the difficulty of the proof of work check or other verifications based on the user's risk score. By using risk scores and adaptive difficulty, you can make it more difficult for attackers to abuse your service while still allowing legitimate users to access it.

## Server-Side Token Verification
Server-side token verification is another critical component of a layered defence strategy. This involves verifying the tokens or keys generated by the service to ensure that they are valid and legitimate. Server-side token verification should be done using a secure protocol, such as HTTPS, and should include strict failure handling to prevent attackers from bypassing the check. It's also essential to use single-use tokens, expiry dates, and error codes to make it more difficult for attackers to abuse the service.

## Logging and Monitoring
Logging and monitoring are essential components of a layered defence strategy. This involves logging and monitoring all signups, requests, and activity on the service to detect and respond to potential abuse. By logging and monitoring this activity, you can identify patterns and trends that may indicate abuse and take action to prevent it. It's also essential to log and monitor the effectiveness of each layer of defence to identify areas for improvement and optimize the defence strategy.

## Implementing a Layered Defence Strategy
Implementing a layered defence strategy requires careful planning and consideration. It's essential to consider the specific needs and requirements of your service and to implement a strategy that is tailored to those needs. This may involve implementing multiple layers of defence, such as proof of work checks, server-side verify checks, rate limits, confirmation, and passkeys. By implementing a layered defence strategy, you can make it more difficult for attackers to abuse your service and protect your users and data.
